◆ § 01 — Manifesto

We've watched a thousand demos. We're done with demos. The bar is a teammate that finishes the ticket and writes a sensible PR description.

Most "AI agents" are
thin wrappers over a chat box.
We teach the rest.

The hardest parts of agent engineering are not in the model. They are in the seams — tool design, retry semantics, eval harnesses, the patient assembly of memory and context, the cold mechanics of cost and latency. The parts no one tweets about.

Automation Agent Academy exists for the people doing the unglamorous work: shipping automation that earns trust, runs on a schedule, and handles the edge cases its operator forgot to mention. No magic. Just craft.

◆ § 02 — Curriculum

Nine weeks. Six modules.
One agent in production.

You'll work in a single repo for the entire program — graduating with a well-instrumented, tested, observable agent of your own design. Each module is two live sessions and a workshop.

  1. 01

    Weeks 1–2

    Foundations & failure modes

    What an agent really is — a control loop with vibes. Tool calling, planning patterns, ReAct vs. graphs, when not to use an agent at all. We dissect five real, failed deployments before writing a line of code.

    4 sessions
  2. 02

    Week 3

    Tool design as product design

    The schema is the prompt. We treat tool surfaces like product APIs — naming, argument shapes, returned context, idempotency. You'll redesign the tools of an agent that's already half-broken.

    2 sessions
  3. 03

    Week 4

    Memory, context, retrieval

    Working memory, episodic logs, retrieval that doesn't lie. Hybrid search, reranking, summarization budgets — and the unglamorous truth that good context wins more bake-offs than bigger models.

    2 sessions
  4. 04

    Weeks 5–6

    Evals, traces & observability

    The work that separates a demo from a deployment. You'll build an eval harness with golden traces, regression gates, and dashboards an on-call engineer can read at 3am.

    4 sessions
  5. 05

    Week 7

    Multi-agent orchestration

    Supervisor patterns, hand-offs, queues, durable workflows. When a graph beats a swarm, when a single agent with patience beats both. We write less code than you'd think.

    2 sessions
  6. 06

    Weeks 8–9

    Ship week & teardown

    Roll-out, guardrails, cost ceilings, kill switches. Each builder presents their agent in a 12-minute teardown to mentors and an invited room of operators. Some get hired in the room.

    4 sessions

◆ § 07 — Questions

Things people
have actually asked.

Still have one? Email hello@aaa.academy — we read every line.

Do I need to be a senior engineer? +

No — but you should be comfortable in a real codebase. The Builder track assumes you've shipped production code in the last 12 months. The Operator track has no code requirement, but you should be the person on your team who specs and accepts the work.

What stack are we using? +

Python or TypeScript — your choice. We use the model providers and orchestration libraries that are useful in May 2026 and we will swap them out the moment they aren't. The point is the patterns, not the framework.

Will my company sponsor this? +

Most do. We provide a one-page brief, an ROI worksheet, and a sample manager email. Roughly 70% of past students were sponsored. We'll happily get on a 15-minute call with your manager.

What's the time commitment, really? +

Plan for 8–12 hours a week. Two live evening sessions (90 minutes each), one critique block, and async work on your project. Ship Week will eat a weekend. We're upfront about it.

What if I don't have a project idea? +

We have a vetted shortlist sourced from cohort partners and alum companies — real problems with real owners. You'll pick one in week one with a mentor's input.

Refund policy? +

Full refund through end of week one — no forms, no friction. After that we'll work with you on a partial refund or a deferral to a later cohort.
